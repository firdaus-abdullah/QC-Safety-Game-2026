Spill Squad

Chemical spill response relay · spill kit, PPE, SDS
Click a pulsing dot on the illustration to reveal a safety insight.

Goal: Teams execute a complete, correct spill response sequence under time pressure — proving the response is muscle memory, not guesswork.

  • Recognise the correct order: protect → identify → contain → clean → dispose → report.
  • Select the right PPE for a spill before contact.
  • Use the SDS to confirm hazard and waste route.
  • 2 mock spill kits (absorbent pads, pillows, tongs, waste bags, caution signs)
  • Blue-coloured water in a squeeze bottle ("chemical")
  • 2 trays, 1 mock SDS sheet per team
  • Nitrile gloves + safety goggles × 2 sets
  • Floor tape to mark a 2-metre contamination zone

Relay format — 10 minutes.

  • P1: dons gloves + goggles correctly, places caution sign, returns.
  • P2: places absorbent pad on the spill, returns.
  • P3: removes broken glass with tongs into the waste bag — never bare hands.
  • P4: wipes the area down.
  • P5: bags and labels the waste for disposal.
  • P6: reads the key hazard + disposal info aloud from the mock SDS.

First team to complete the full sequence correctly wins.

Complete correct sequence+10
Bare-hand contact−5
Missing or incorrect PPE−5
Wrong waste disposal route−5
Fastest safe completion+5
2

PPE Speed & Spot

Dress right, spot wrong · donning order & violation ID
Click a pulsing dot on the illustration to reveal a safety insight.

Goal: Prove that everyone knows the correct donning and doffing order — and can spot a PPE violation in a photograph at a glance.

  • Correct sequence: hairnet → mask → goggles → coat → gloves.
  • Doffing removes the most contaminated item first, gloves last-but-one.
  • Build the habit of scanning colleagues before entering the lab.
  • 2 full PPE sets: lab coat, goggles, nitrile gloves, hairnet, shoe covers, mask
  • 10 printed "PPE violation" photos (open-toe shoes, gloves on a door handle, goggles on forehead, loose hair near equipment, etc.)
  • 2 boards or clear table areas
  • Sticky flags and markers for each team

Two rounds — 10 minutes.

  • Round 1 — Speed Donning (5 min): one player per team dons full PPE in the correct order while teammates coach. Then they doff in the correct order. Accuracy is judged over speed.
  • Round 2 — Spot the Violation (5 min): teams receive 10 photos and have 3 minutes to flag every violation and write down what is wrong.
Correct donning + doffing order+10
Each correctly identified violation+2
Wrong order step−2
Safety stop (bare hands / no goggles)−5
3

Broken Glass Protocol

Breakage handling · sharps disposal · cracked glassware
Click a pulsing dot on the illustration to reveal a safety insight.

Goal: Turn breakage cleanup into a planned, rehearsed procedure rather than an improvised scramble.

  • Plan the cleanup as a toolbox talk before touching anything.
  • Handle "contaminated glass" twist scenarios correctly.
  • Reinforce that cracked glassware is removed from service, not reused.
  • 2 sets: cut-resistant gloves, dustpan, brush, tongs, thick paper towel
  • 2 mock sharps containers and biohazard bags
  • Rigid plastic "shards" or crumpled foil — never real glass
  • 6 twist cards (contaminated glass, cracked beaker, glass in a sink, etc.)
  • Stopwatch and whistle

Timed challenge — 10 minutes.

  • Planning phase (3 min): team writes and agrees the cleanup sequence — exactly like a pre-task toolbox talk.
  • Execution: two players perform the cleanup under judge observation: cut-resistant gloves → tongs/dustpan (never bare hands) → wrap in thick paper → designated container → inspect area → report breakage.
  • Twist card: the judge interrupts mid-cleanup with a scenario — e.g. "This glass contained a hazardous chemical — what now?" or "You found a crack in another beaker — do you use it?"
Correct cleanup sequence+10
Correct twist-card response+5
Fastest safe cleanup+5
Any bare-hand motion (safety stop)−10
4

Cylinder Shuffle

Compressed gas · inspection, securing, transport, emergency
Click a pulsing dot on the illustration to reveal a safety insight.

Goal: Rehearse the full cylinder lifecycle — inspect, secure, move, store — and practise the two emergency responses teams get wrong most often.

  • Inspect before you move: cap, valve, body damage, label.
  • Always upright, always secured, always with a trolley or sling.
  • Hissing cylinder or falling cylinder: evacuate and escalate — do not intervene.
  • 2 empty cylinders or PVC pipe props (~60 cm) with a rope handle
  • Cylinder trolley or rope sling, chain/clamp, mock wall bracket
  • 5–6 cones for the obstacle course
  • "NO SMOKING" / "OXIDIZER" / "FLAMMABLE" signage cards

Obstacle relay — 10 minutes.

  • P1: inspects the cylinder (cap on, no damage, label correct) and shouts "CLEAR".
  • P2: secures it upright on the trolley or sling — judge checks.
  • P3 & P4: navigate the cone course together. No dragging, no rolling on its side, never left unsecured.
  • P5: chains it to the wall bracket upright and places the correct signage.
  • Judge triggers: "The cylinder is hissing!" and "The cylinder has fallen!" — correct response: evacuate, ventilate, remove ignition sources, notify.
Safe transport technique+10
Correct securing to bracket+5
Correct signage placement+5
Each correct emergency response+5
Dragging, rolling or leaving unsecured−10
5

Label Lockdown

Sample ID · chain of custody · segregation · quarantine
Click a pulsing dot on the illustration to reveal a safety insight.

Goal: Make the link between a sloppy label and a downstream safety event — then make correct labelling and segregation the default.

  • Accept or reject a sample based on label completeness.
  • Segregate by compatibility without mixing incompatible chemicals.
  • Treat labelling as a patient-safety issue, not paperwork.
  • 20 mock sample bottles with a mix of correct and defective labels (missing date, illegible name, wrong hazard symbol, unlabelled, expired)
  • 3 sorting bins labelled Acids / Bases & Solvents / Rejected–Quarantine
  • Red "REJECT" and white "ACCEPT" stickers for each team
  • 2 surprise bottles with deliberately wrong hazard symbols

Two-phase race — 10 minutes.

  • Phase 1 — Label Inspection (5 min): each team receives 10 bottles and must ACCEPT or REJECT every one based on label completeness. Rejected bottles get a red sticker and a written reason.
  • Phase 2 — Segregation Sprint (5 min): accepted bottles are placed in the correct compatibility bin. The judge swaps in two surprise bottles with wrong hazard symbols mid-game.
Each correct accept/reject decision+2
Each correct written reason+1
Each correct bin placement+3
Mixing incompatible chemicals−10
